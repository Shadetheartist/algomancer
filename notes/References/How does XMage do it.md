# XMage

XMage is an open source GRE for magic. It does run. People do use it. It basically works. 

Looks like it was designed by programmers. Overall horrible UX, especially for the modern user. Bad use of space. Sound design is awful.

It's not doing too hot in terms of stability. Likely due to its open-source nature. Developers are mostly concerned with the game functioning correctly, it's way to much work to pull off a redesign. Doesn't come with images, probably due to legal reasons. 

Overall terrible experience. But still they have put together something that works well enough. And it actually has a lot of features, which is both a boon and a bane. Each feature is individually calling for attention in the UI, and it's so cluttered as to be repulsive as a result. 

This project provides a lot of useful info on how *not* to go about maintaining an open source project if your goal is a high quality product at the end.

## Is there any hope?

XMage attempts the (increasingly) impossible in daring to create a MTG GRE. As a project i think it's actually quite successful. The problem is that MTG is a quickly expanding domain and no one is being paid to keep the GRE at pace. In comparison, WOTC's MTGA has a host of talented developers and QA staff, and even still, MTGA still has bugs and shortfalls.

However for Algomancy, we are dealing with vastly fewer cards, and the landscape isn't shifting constantly beneath our feet as we try to develop the client. However each card in Algomancy is *interesting and unique*. So even with relatively few cards, there are still many mechanics introduced by the game, which all need implementation.
